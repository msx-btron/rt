Each RISC-V width prints four tables, in this order. The paste stops after the second.

1. Scalar, vectorize off. src/common/scalar/, compiled -march=rv32gc -fno-vectorize.
   The C copies aligned size_t words (lw/sw on RV32, ld/sd on RV64).
   That is the first table, the one that ends at scalar32_off_exit:0.

2. Scalar, vectorize on. The same files, with -march=rv32gcv and -DRT_VECTORIZE.
   The word loops are compiled out and Clang emits RVV (vsetvli, vle8.v, vse8.v).
   The build rejects this object if those opcodes are missing.

3. RVV assembly. src/riscv32/rvv/ or src/riscv64/rvv/.

4. Integer assembly. src/riscv32/rv32/mem.s or src/riscv64/rv64/mem.s, banner rv32 or rv64.
   Memory rows only. On the RV32 guest that table was memcpy 0.85, memmove 0.76, memset 0.64, memcmp 3.26, 53234 checks, fail 0.
   Those nanoseconds are the QEMU guest clock.
